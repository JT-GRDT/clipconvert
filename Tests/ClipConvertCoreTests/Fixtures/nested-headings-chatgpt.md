## Militarism

 - European powers expanded their armies and navies.
- Germany and Britain competed heavily in naval construction.
- Military planning and alliances made governments more prepared for war.

 ## Alliances

 - Europe became divided into two major alliance systems:
  - **Triple Alliance:** Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy.
  - **Triple Entente:** Britain, France, and Russia.
- Alliances increased the risk that a conflict involving two countries would spread to others.

 ## Imperialism

 - European powers competed for colonies, resources, and influence.
- Rivalries, particularly between Germany, Britain, and France, increased international tensions.

 ## Nationalism

 - Strong national identities encouraged rivalry between European states.
- In the Balkans, Slavic nationalism challenged Austria-Hungary's control.
- Serbian nationalism was particularly important to tensions in the region.

 ## Balkan Crises

 - The decline of the Ottoman Empire created competition for territory and influence.
- Austria-Hungary and Serbia had opposing interests in the Balkans.
- The Balkan Wars of 1912–13 further increased regional tensions.

 ## Assassination of Franz Ferdinand

 - On **28 June 1914**, Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary was assassinated in Sarajevo by **Gavrilo Princip**, a Bosnian Serb nationalist.
- Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and issued an ultimatum.
- The resulting crisis activated the alliance system and led to declarations of war in July and August 1914.

 ## Immediate Cause vs. Deeper Causes

 - The assassination was the **immediate trigger**, rather than the sole cause.
- Militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism, and Balkan tensions had created an unstable political environment in which the crisis could escalate into a wider European war.
