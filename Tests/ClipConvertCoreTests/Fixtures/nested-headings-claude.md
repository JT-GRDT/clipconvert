# Causes of World War I

## 1. Militarism
- European powers engaged in an arms race, particularly Germany and Britain competing over naval power.
- Large standing armies and glorification of military strength created a culture primed for conflict.
- Military planning (like Germany's Schlieffen Plan) locked nations into rigid strategies that favored fast mobilization over diplomacy.

## 2. Alliances
- Europe was divided into two main power blocs: the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and the Triple Entente (France, Russia, Britain).
- These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly pull in others.
- Countries felt obligated to support their allies, turning regional disputes into continental ones.

## 3. Imperialism
- European powers competed fiercely for colonies and global influence, especially in Africa and Asia.
- This competition bred resentment and rivalry, particularly between Britain, France, and Germany.
- Imperial ambitions heightened nationalist pride and economic competition.

## 4. Nationalism
- Strong nationalist sentiment across Europe fueled tensions, especially in the Balkans.
- Slavic nationalism in Serbia clashed directly with Austro-Hungarian imperial interests.
- Ethnic groups within empires (like Austria-Hungary) sought independence, destabilizing existing power structures.

## 5. The Balkans as a "Powder Keg"
- The Balkan region was a hotbed of ethnic tension and competing interests among Austria-Hungary, Russia, and the Ottoman Empire's declining influence.
- Two Balkan Wars (1912–1913) had already heightened instability before 1914.
- Serbia's growing power and Slavic nationalism worried Austria-Hungary.

## 6. The Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand
- The immediate trigger: Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary was assassinated by Gavrilo Princip, a Bosnian Serb nationalist, on June 28, 1914.
- Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and issued an ultimatum with harsh demands.
- Serbia's response, while conciliatory, wasn't enough to prevent Austria-Hungary from declaring war.
- This set off the chain reaction of alliance obligations, rapidly escalating a regional conflict into a world war.

**In short:** long-term tensions (militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism) created a volatile environment, while the assassination in Sarajevo served as the spark that ignited the powder keg.
